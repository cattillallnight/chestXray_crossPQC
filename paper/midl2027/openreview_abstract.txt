A hybrid quantum-classical head is often scored as if it were a full image classifier. That comparison confounds the circuit with the backbone, and it is the usual way to claim that a parameterized quantum circuit (PQC) "detects" disease on chest radiographs. We ask a smaller, answerable question. Freeze an ImageNet DenseNet-121, compress its features to six angles, and train three single-logit heads of nearly equal nominal size---a multilayer perceptron (MLP), a product PQC, and a ring-CNOT PQC. Models trained on CheXpert are tested on VinDr-CXR; the primary question is calibration, not ranking. We also report a methodological finding that is easy to miss: with a single-qubit readout <Z0> and no entangling gates, the product circuit is functionally a one-feature model, because only z0 reaches the measured wire. The product-versus-entangling contrast therefore changes two things at once (entanglement and input routing), and matched nominal parameter counts do not imply matched functional capacity. Under this protocol neither PQC head is a more trustworthy classifier than the matched MLP: entanglement does not reduce expected calibration error, discrimination is comparable or slightly worse, and a temperature fitted on CheXpert validation can lower source error while raising target error on the same run. On both balanced and natural-prevalence VinDr-CXR holdouts these conclusions hold. VinDr-CXR has no Pneumonia class; that ontology change is confined to the appendix.
