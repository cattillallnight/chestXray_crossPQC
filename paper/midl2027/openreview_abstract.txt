A hybrid quantum-classical head is often scored as if it were a full image classifier. That comparison confounds the circuit with the backbone, and it is the usual way to claim that a parameterized quantum circuit (PQC) "detects" disease on chest radiographs. This paper asks a smaller question that the implementation can actually answer. We freeze an ImageNet DenseNet-121, compress its features to six angles, and train three heads of nearly equal size---a multilayer perceptron (MLP), a product PQC, and a ring-CNOT PQC---to emit a single logit. The heads see the same bottleneck; they never see pixels. Models trained on CheXpert are then tested on VinDr-CXR. Discrimination is secondary. The primary question is whether the PQC inductive bias, with or without entanglement, yields better-calibrated probabilities under that geographic shift. In this protocol it does not consistently. Entanglement does not reduce expected calibration error. A temperature fitted on CheXpert validation can lower source error and raise target error on the same run. VinDr-CXR has no Pneumonia class; that ontology change is left in the appendix. With a frozen ImageNet DenseNet-121, a shared six-dimensional bottleneck, six-qubit two-layer heads, three seeds, and a noiseless default.qubit simulator, neither PQC head is a more trustworthy replacement for the matched MLP.
