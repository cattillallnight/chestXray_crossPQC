Heads are trained with Adam (Kingma and Ba, 2015) (lr = 10−3, weight decay 10−4),
binary cross-entropy with logits, 40 epochs, and early stopping of 8 epochs on validation
AUROC. MLP batches are size 64 on CUDA when available; PQC batches are size 16 on
CPU. Seeds are 42, 43, and 44. The same CheXpert validation split is then reused to fit a
scalar temperature T = exp(θ) by L-BFGS on validation logits (Guo et al., 2017); CheXpert
test and VinDr-CXR are never used for model selection or for T.

4. Experiments
Forty-five of forty-five scheduled runs finished. VinDr metrics are present for every run.
Mean ± standard deviation is over three seeds. Discrimination is in Table 2; calibration is
in Table 3; paired differences versus the matched MLP are in Table 4. Figure 3 shows the
same means with seed error bars. The prose below states the pattern; the tables carry the
numbers.
4.1. Discrimination is comparable, not better
On CheXpert test data the MLP is at least as discriminative as either PQC on every
primary task (Table 2). The cleanest ontology is no finding: the three heads sit within a
few points of one another, with the MLP slightly ahead. Under VinDr balanced evaluation
the ranking is not reversed in a way that would support a quantum story. Product PQC
is not uniformly worse on the target—consolidation is the one cell where it is numerically
higher—but that gap sits inside seed noise. Across the 12 primary task–seed pairs, product
AUROC is below the matched MLP on 9 CheXpert tests and entangling AUROC is below
the MLP on 10. Mean paired ∆AUROC versus MLP is negative for both heads on the
source (Table 4). Three seeds do not support a hierarchical significance test; the pattern is
reported as paired mean differences, not as p-values.
