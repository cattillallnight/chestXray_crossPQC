Abstract
A hybrid quantum-classical head is often scored as if it were a full image classifier. That
comparison confounds the circuit with the backbone, and it is the usual way to claim
that a parameterized quantum circuit (PQC) “detects” disease on chest radiographs. This
paper asks a smaller question that the implementation can actually answer. We freeze
an ImageNet DenseNet-121, compress its features to six angles, and train three heads
of nearly equal size—a multilayer perceptron (MLP), a product PQC, and a ring-CNOT
PQC—to emit a single logit. The heads see the same bottleneck; they never see pixels.
Models trained on CheXpert are then tested on VinDr-CXR. Discrimination is secondary.
The primary question is whether the PQC inductive bias, with or without entanglement,
yields better-calibrated probabilities under that geographic shift. In this protocol it does
not consistently. Entanglement does not reduce expected calibration error. A temperature
fitted on CheXpert validation can lower source error and raise target error on the same run.
VinDr-CXR has no Pneumonia class; that ontology change is left in the appendix. With
a frozen ImageNet DenseNet-121, a shared six-dimensional bottleneck, six-qubit two-layer
heads, three seeds, and a noiseless default.qubit simulator, neither PQC head is a more
trustworthy replacement for the matched MLP.

1. Introduction
A chest-radiograph classifier that looks well calibrated in one hospital can become over-
or under-confident in another (Ovadia et al., 2019; Zech et al., 2018). Calibration under
shift is therefore a property of the deployed model, not only of the training loss. In paral-
lel, variational circuits have been attached to classical backbones and described as hybrid
quantum-classical networks (Benedetti et al., 2019; Cerezo et al., 2021). Many of those
studies compare a small PQC with a fully trained convolutional classifier, or they map
CheXpert Pneumonia onto VinDr classes that do not include Pneumonia.
Both moves
credit the circuit for work it did not do.
The comparison that isolates the circuit is narrower. Keep the representation fixed.
