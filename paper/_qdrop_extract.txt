Q-Drop: Optimizing Quantum Orthogonal Networks
with Statistic Pruning and Dynamic Dropout
Pham Thai Quang Nguyen ∗, Tran Cat Khanh ∗, Y ared Abera Ergu ∗, V an-Linh Nguyen∗
∗Dept. of Computer Science and Information Engineering, National Chung Cheng University, Chiayi, Taiwan

Abstract—Quantum machine learning (QML) holds immense
potential for revolutionizing computational intelligence, yet faces
signiﬁcant challenges in optimizing quantum neural network
architectures for practical implementation. This paper intro-
duces Q-Drop, an innovative quantum adaptation technique
that enhances parameterized quantum circuits (PQCs) for train-
ing performance. The system includes two novel optimization
strategies: statistical pruning and dynamic dropout (circuit ﬂip-
ﬂops), which signiﬁcantly improve the learning performance and
robustness of quantum orthogonal neural networks. Evaluation
results across standard (MNIST, Fashion-MNIST) and medical
imaging (Pneumonia-MNIST, Retina-MNIST) datasets demon-
strate substantial performance gains. Notably, the approach
achieves up to 94.3% accuracy on medical image classiﬁcation
and 98.7% accuracy on standard image datasets, outperforming
existing quantum machine learning methods. By addressing
critical challenges in quantum neural network training, this work
highlights the potential of applying quantum machine learning
to enhance performance for classical computer vision tasks and
QML-driven scheduling optimization in quantum networks.
Index Terms —Quantum machine learning, Orthogonal neu-
ral network, Image classiﬁcation, Statistical pruning, Dynamic
dropout
I. I NTRODUCTION
Quantum computing is a rapidly evolving ﬁeld that lever-
ages principles of quantum mechanics, such as superposition
and entanglement, to unlock computational possibilities be-
yond classical limits [1]. At its core, quantum computing
aims to tackle problems that remain intractable for classi-
cal methods, including certain types of optimization, high-
dimensional data processing, and complex pattern recognition.
Quantum computings power spans healthcare, cryptography,
and space exploration, driving breakthroughs in drug discov-
ery, personalized medicine, and secure communications like
quantum key distribution (QKD) [2], [3]. Quantum Machine
Learning (QML) merges quantum computing with machine
learning, potentially addressing the computational limitations
of conventional deep learning (DL) [4]. While DL models
often struggle with high-dimensional feature spaces, model
complexity, and data sparsity, QML may offer solutions by
leveraging quantum states to represent and process information
compactly. This compact representation could allow QML
algorithms to explore vast search spaces more efﬁciently and
accelerate convergence on complex problems that challenge
DL due to computational bottlenecks. However, it is crucial
to recognize that QML may not inherently resolve hardware-

B. Dynamic quantum circuit dropout mechanism
We propose a new quantum dropout mechanism, termed
Q-Drop. This technique provides a promising approach to
managing the complexity of quantum circuits during hybrid
quantum-classical training. Inspired by classical dropout meth-
ods ﬁrst introduced in [14], Q-Drop aims to mitigate over-
ﬁtting by randomly deactivating a subset of straight RBS gates
(connected to one wire) in some wires during both the forward
and backward phases of training. This strategy discounts the
transforms used in some qubits and helps the model achieve
a balance between generalization and the preservation of
critical information ﬂow. In particular, by dropping certain
RBS gates associated with speciﬁc qubits, we can identify
the least impactful qubits in the training process. This en-
courages the model to explore a broader range of parameter
combinations, ultimately leading to improved convergence.
Furthermore, given the limited number of qubits available in
current quantum computers, which are often subject to high

freezing in neural networks [15], [16], we propose a gradient-

based pruning approach for quantum learning networks. Our
method, inspired by [17], leverages accumulated gradients
to identify and preserve critical updates during the quantum
circuit’s learning process. The approach is based on the obser-
vation that gradient magnitude patterns tend to be persistent:
parameters with consistently signiﬁcant gradients typically re-
main important, while those with consistently small gradients
tend to stay negligible. This predictability makes gradient
accumulation an effective metric for parameter importance.
However, as training approaches optimum, the decreased
standard deviation of accumulated gradient magnitudes can
make parameter pruning potentially disruptive. We address
this through an adaptive scheduler that dynamically adjusts
the pruning ratio based on training progress.
The naive algorithm described in Algorithm 2 differs from
the computer vision dropout technique, as it only skips the
backward updates, leaving the forward path intact. The algo-
rithm implements this strategy through two main components.
The ﬁrst component (lines 2-11) handles the control mech-
anism, which manages the pruning ﬂag and window-based
scheduling. The pruning ﬂag alternates between accumulation
and pruning phases based on predeﬁned window sizes (W a,
Wp). During execution, the parameter θis updated as usual,
and gradients are accumulated. In contrast, the pruning phase
rescales the normalization of the accumulated gradients and se-
lectively prunes based on statistical sampling. The pruning step
begins by normalizing the accumulated gradients, computing
logits for categorical sampling, and selecting parameter indices
to prune based on the prune ratio. A mask is created to retain
only the selected gradients, which are then applied to quantum
weights, while other gradients update the remaining variables.
The accumulated gradients are reset for the next window.
At each window transition, the algorithm resets accumulated
states and adjusts the pruning rate using an exponential decay
formula, ensuring a gradual reduction in pruning intensity.

III. E XPERIMENT RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
A. Experimental Setup
The experiment includes four distinct datasets tailored for
binary classiﬁcation tasks. These include: (1) MNIST [18] is
used for two binary classiﬁcation tasks: one target to classify
digits ’3’ from ’6’ and another targeting digits ’0’ and ’1’ ; (2)
Fashion-MNIST [19] is utilized for two binary classiﬁcation
tasks: one aims to distinguish dresses from shirts (class 0 and
class 3), while the other differentiates class shirts from class
trousers (class 0 and class 1); (3) Pneumonia-MNIST [20]
which consists of pediatric chest X-ray images, and (4) Retina-
MNIST derived from the DeepDRiD [21] dataset, aimed at
identifying varying levels of retinopathy against normal retinal
images. Speciﬁcally, Retina-MNIST was adapted for a binary
classiﬁcation task, segregating normal retinal images (class
0) from images depicting different severities of retinopathy
(classes 1 to 4). For the MNIST (3 and 6) and Fashion-MNIST
2-class (shirts and dresses) datasets, our training set comprised
the initial 500 images, while the validation set was formed
by randomly selecting 300 images. All input images were
initially 28 × 28 pixels in size. We processed these images

C. Discussion on the weaknesses and calibration
Our new techniques, while offering more control and po-
tentially better performance through additional hyperparame-
ters, also introduce training complexities. Excessive pruning
or dropping gates can degrade performance, especially in
later training stages. To address this, we must carefully ﬁne-
tune these parameters, reducing their inﬂuence as training
progresses, resembling a learning rate schedule. Our exper-
iments with the quantum dropout method reveal signiﬁcant
accuracy improvements in image classiﬁcation tasks, provided
the dropout gates remain below 15% of the total. For the
statistical pruning technique, we observed an effective range
between 30% and 70% of the accumulated gradients being
selectively updated, depending on the speciﬁc dataset. This
selective gradient update strategy allows the model to focus on
the most important parameters while reducing the pruning rate
as training progresses, leading to better overall performance.
IV . CONCLUSION
In this study, we introduced two novel techniques to en-
hance quantum orthogonal networks: quantum statistic prun-
ing and dynamic dropout. Our experimental results across
four datasets: MNIST, FashionMNIST, PneumoniaMNIST,
and RetinaMNIST showed signiﬁcant improvements in ac-
curacy and robustness. Speciﬁcally, the statistical pruning
technique accelerates training convergence while maintaining
model stability, whereas dynamic dropout effectively reduces
overﬁtting by selectively deactivating qubits. These ﬁndings
underscore the potential of optimization methodologies for
quantum machine learning-driven functions. This work con-
tributes to the evolving ﬁeld of quantum machine learning
optimization, enhancing the stability and application ability of
quantum-driven systems.
